SPELL SEAL

BB84 quantum key distribution · an eavesdropper leaves a statistical scar

Bennett–Brassard 1984. Alice encodes each random bit in a random basis (Z or X); Bob measures each qubit in a random basis; they publicly compare bases and keep the sifted bits. In this ideal simulation, with no Eve, the quantum bit error rate on the sifted key is exactly 0. An intercept-resend eavesdropper who measures in a random basis and forwards the collapsed state induces QBER = 25% on the sifted key (she guesses the basis half the time; when she is wrong she fully randomizes Bob’s bit). A threshold of 11% is the usual textbook abort for this attack family — if QBER is above it, they discard the key. This page is a teaching toy: it has no authenticated classical channel, no privacy amplification, and no finite-key security proof. It does not make an unbreakable magical seal. Real QKD security is a theorem about a protocol, not a vibe.
